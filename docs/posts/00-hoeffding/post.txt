X and Y are independent exactly when their joint CDF factorises at every point:
F_XY(x,y) − F_X(x)F_Y(y) = 0 for all (x,y).

Hoeffding (1940) showed that the covariance is the integral of the same difference:
Cov(X,Y) = ∬ [F_XY(x,y) − F_X(x)F_Y(y)] dx dy

So independence requires the difference to vanish everywhere, while zero covariance requires only that its positive and negative parts cancel. If it has a fixed sign, zero covariance does imply independence (Lehmann, 1966).
