Hoeffding's covariance identity (The Quiet Theorems No. 00): https://omidsolari.substack.com/p/probability-notes-no-00-hoeffdings
Plotted: X uniform on {−1, 0, 1} and Y = X². The two squares cancel, so Cov(X,Y) = 0 although Y is a function of X.

Hoeffding (1940) showed that
Cov(X,Y) = ∬ [F_XY(x,y) − F_X(x)F_Y(y)] dx dy
whenever X, Y and XY have finite means. The bracket is the joint CDF minus the product of the marginals.

X and Y are independent exactly when the bracket is zero everywhere. Zero covariance asks much less: only that its positive and negative parts cancel, as in the plot.

If the bracket has a fixed sign, zero covariance does imply independence (Lehmann, 1966).
